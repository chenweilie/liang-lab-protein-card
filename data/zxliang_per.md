## Research Interests and Focus Areas

According to NTU‑linked biographies and publication lists, Dr. Liang’s research interests are broadly summarized as **“the interface of chemistry and biology.”** More specifically, his group focuses on several interconnected topics: [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)

- **Cyclic dinucleotide signaling in bacteria**  
  - Synthesis, recognition, and functional roles of cyclic di‑adenosine monophosphate (c‑di‑AMP) and cyclic di‑guanosine monophosphate (c‑di‑GMP) as second messengers in bacteria. [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)
  - How these small molecules are produced and degraded by specific enzymes, and how they regulate cellular processes such as cell wall biosynthesis, motility, and chemotaxis via binding proteins and adaptor proteins. [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)

- **Protein–protein and protein–ligand interactions in signaling**  
  - Structural and mechanistic studies of adaptor proteins that bridge cyclic dinucleotides to histidine kinases and chemotaxis methyltransferases, thereby modulating two‑component systems and flagellar motor switching. [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)

- **Nucleic acid structure and thermodynamics (earlier career work)**  
  - Experimental and thermodynamic analysis of DNA hairpin loops, including the effects of loop size, sequence, mismatches, and bulges on stability, as well as enthalpy–entropy compensation and hydration contributions. [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/bcsj/article-abstract/88/9/1314/7344582)

- **Enzyme engineering and natural product biosynthesis**  
  - Discovery and characterization of novel enzymes from biosynthetic pathways such as anthraquinone‑fused enediynes, often presented in the context of enzyme engineering conferences. [proceedings](https://www.proceedings.com/content/073/073683webtoc.pdf)

## Representative Publications

From his NTU‑affiliated biography and related bibliographic sources, several publications stand out as representative of his research program:  

- **Cyclic di‑AMP and cell wall biosynthesis**  
  - “Cyclic di‑AMP synthesis by the diadenylate cyclase CdaA is modulated by the peptidoglycan biosynthesis enzyme GlmM in *Lactococcus lactis*.”  
    - Zhu, Y., Pham, T. H., Nhiep, T. N. H., Marcellin, E., Chakrabortti, A., Vu, N. M. T., Wang, Y., Waanders, J., Bansal, N., Nielsen, L. K., Liang, Z.‑X., Turner, M.   - This work shows how the diadenylate cyclase CdaA, responsible for c‑di‑AMP production, is functionally coupled to the peptidoglycan biosynthesis enzyme GlmM, revealing a direct link between nucleotide signaling and cell wall metabolism. [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)

- **c‑di‑GMP‑binding adaptor proteins and two‑component systems**  
  - “A cyclic di‑GMP‑binding adaptor protein interacts with histidine kinase to regulate two‑component signaling.”  
    - Xu, L., Venkataramani, P., Ding, Y., Yong, G., Liu, Y., Deng, Y., Xin, L., Ye, Y., Yang, L., Liang, Z.‑X., *Journal of Biological Chemistry* (2016). [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)
  - This paper describes a c‑di‑GMP‑binding adaptor protein that modulates a histidine kinase, providing a molecular mechanism for how c‑di‑GMP can rewire classic two‑component signaling modules. [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)

- **c‑di‑GMP and chemotaxis control**  
  - “A c‑di‑GMP‑binding adaptor protein directly interacts with a chemotaxis methyltransferase to control flagellar motor switching.”  
    - Xu, L. H., Xing, L. Y., Zeng, Y., Yam, J. K. H., Ding, Y., Venkataramani, P., Cheang, Q. W., Yang, X., Tang, X., Zhang, L.‑H., Chiam, K.‑H., Yang, L., Liang, Z.‑X., *Science Signaling* (2016). [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)
  - This study links c‑di‑GMP levels to chemotactic behavior by showing that the adaptor protein binds a chemotaxis methyltransferase, thereby influencing flagellar motor switching and bacterial movement. [biography.omicsonline](https://biography.omicsonline.org/singapore/nanyang-technological-university/liang-zhaoxun-761121)

- **DNA hairpin thermodynamics (early work)**  
  - “Alternative Method for Evaluating Stabilities of DNA Hairpin Loops Using T4 DNA Ligase.”  
    - In this and related work, Dr. Liang contributed to methods that use ligase‑mediated reactions to probe hairpin loop stability over a range of loop sizes and sequences. [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/bcsj/article-abstract/88/9/1314/7344582)
  - “Folding of Intramolecular DNA Hairpin Loops: Enthalpy–Entropy Compensation and Hydration Effects.”  
    - This study quantified thermodynamic parameters governing hairpin folding, helping clarify how hydration and base composition tune hairpin stability. [pubs.acs](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/jp0260853)

These publications together define a coherent line of research from nucleic acid structure to complex bacterial signaling networks controlled by cyclic dinucleotides.
